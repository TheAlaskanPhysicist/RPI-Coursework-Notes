Humans should choose what an AI is allowed and not allowed to do, and is accountable for it.
Students have higher homework scores if they use AI, but tends to be a detriment when exams come around. I think that's more an indication that the scoring methods if flawed, even if I find using AI on an exam as morally reprehensible.

Languages choices lead to real effects.
1. the change in your voice and confidence isn't matching
2. Word choice and tone matter
3. Authentic voice matters to maintain clarity and parity
4. Grammar-editing tools often choose for you
5. Language depends on context and audience
6. There's not just 1 way to write things in English.

LLMs train on a lot of sources. You have to evaluate the output to see if it's reasonable. Don't outsource your thinking. Value for future employers is what you can do that AI *can't* do. You are responsible for the final output. When you use AI, think about reputation, client trust, and ability to secure future work.

There's also a significant number of copywrite lawsuits. AI scraping data off the internet and never gets permission pretty much. What you disclose to AI is considered your intellectual property. Don't give it everything so it doesn't steal anything.

AI is a fluent liar. It makes sentences that looks right but it's not based in reality.

How to make a good prompt: S.P.A.R.K.
1. Say it simply
2. Provide details
3. Add context 
4. Request accuracy
5. Keep it honest

AI may assist in drafting, but it can't replace scientific judgement or data ownership.
